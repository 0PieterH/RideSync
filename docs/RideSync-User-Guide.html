RideSync — User Guide

A friendly, start-to-finish guide to putting music, voices and sound effects on a NoLimits 2 coaster that play on the train as it moves — launch music, station loops, voice lines (screams, narration) and hold music.

1 · What is this?

NoLimits 2 can't play audio timed to a moving train on its own. RideSync adds it:

  • RideSync — a desktop app (looks like a simple audio timeline) where you lay out your sounds and decide when each one plays.
  • The Audio Package — the editor exports a small data file plus a script that you drop into your NL2 park folder so the coaster plays your audio.

You do not need to know how to program. If you can drag clips around a timeline and tick a few boxes, you can do this.

The one rule to remember“Cues” play in the game — not the clips you see on the timeline. Clips are your raw material; a Cue is what actually plays a sound. You'll build clips first, then wrap them in cues.

2 · What you need

  • A Windows PC.
  • NoLimits 2 with a coaster you can attach a script to.
  • Your audio files (.wav or .ogg), anywhere on your PC — you point RideSync at them when adding audio.
  • The RideSync app. You can run the ready-made portable app — no installation required.

If you're building the app from source instead, you'll also need Node.js 18 or newer.

3 · Setup

3.1 Start the editor

Double-click the app — the portable .exe, or Launch RideSync.bat in the project folder. The editor opens with an empty timeline.

3.2 Set the basics

Open Settings → Project and check:

SettingWhat to put
Timeline lengthHow long the whole show is (seconds). A little longer than your ride is fine.
All trains (each independent)Tick to make the audio run independently for every train (one script for the whole coaster). Leave off to target a single train.
Train indexWhich single train this audio targets. Use 0 for the first train. Disabled when "All trains" is ticked.
Script nameLeave as TimelineAudio unless you run a separate script per train.
Timecode frame rateCosmetic only — how times are displayed. 30 fps is fine.
TipIf your park uses a scripted coaster (block states), tick Settings → Project → “Scripted coaster”. It's off by default; when off, the app uses NL2's built-in block states.

4 · Quick start (5 minutes)

Goal: make a station loop start when the train arrives, and launch music start when the train leaves.

Three words firstA clip is one sound on the timeline. A cue is the thing that actually plays in-game. A trigger is what tells a cue to start — a track marker, a block state, or a time.
  1. Add your audio. Use the More menu (the “+” button, top right) → Add audio, or drag .wav files onto the timeline. Each file becomes a clip.
  2. Add a cue. Open the Cues menu (top toolbar) and press +. A new cue appears.
  3. Choose what triggers it. In the properties panel, set Triggered by to Track trigger and type a name, e.g. station. (In NL2 you'll place a track trigger named audio_trigger_station — the app also matches a trigger with the exact name you typed.)
  4. Make it a Clip Cue. Set Cue type to Clip Cue. Position the cue's start and end times so its window covers the clips you want it to play.
  5. Optional: loop it. For background music, choose Play mode → Loop. Leave it as Once for a sound that plays a single time.
  6. Add a launch cue. Repeat: new cue, Triggered by → Block state, and enter a block name and state (for example Zone1 / 4 — use your coaster's own names). Point it at your launch music.
  7. If you looped any music (step 6), add a Stop Cue. New cue → Cue type → Stop Cue, same trigger as the launch. This fades out those loops when the next sound takes over — e.g. the station music dipping out as the launch music starts. Set its Fade time (e.g. 3 s).
  8. Export. More menu → Export Audio Package. It bundles the game file, the script, the resource list and all your audio into one .zip.
  9. Deploy. Unzip it into your park's RideSync folder (see section 10), attach the script, and test in NL2.

That's the whole loop of work: clips → cues → export → deploy → test.

Left panel — Tracks

Lanes that hold your clips. Tracks are just for organising your work; they don't change how the game plays audio.

  • Rename — click the track name and type.
  • Color — click the colour swatch.
  • Mute (M) / Solo (S) — for preview only; they don't change the exported audio.
  • Remove — the × on the track (the last track can't be removed).
  • Reorder — drag a track up or down.
  • Groups — add one with + Group; drag a track onto a group to assign it, or onto empty space to ungroup. Groups are organisation only.

You can have up to 30 tracks.

Centre — Timeline

Your clips and cues laid out in time. Drag clips to move them; drag their right edge to shorten (trim) them. Drag on empty timeline space to box-select several clips, and Ctrl/Shift-click to add clips to the selection — dragging any selected clip moves them all together.

Right panel — Properties

Shows the settings for whatever you've selected: a clip, a cue, or a sound trigger. This is where most of the work happens.

Sound Triggers

A small list of one-shot sounds that a track trigger can fire directly (e.g. a single scream or horn). Add one with the +; select it to edit its name, file, play mode and volume (see section 8). Optional — many rides don't need them.

6 · Core concepts (plain language)

TermMeaning
ClipOne piece of audio on the timeline. Doesn't play by itself in-game.
TrackA lane for clips. Organisation only.
CueThe thing that actually plays in-game. One cue = one sound event.
Clip CueA cue that plays the clips inside its time window (layered and/or in sequence).
Stop CueA cue that fades out and stops looping cues (used when the ride begins).
File CueA cue that plays one audio file directly. Set Cue type → File Cue, then pick the sound in the Audio file dropdown.
Sound TriggerA named one-shot sound fired by a track trigger.
RoleHow the sound behaves: Normal (no special behaviour), Music (dips under voices and fades out for hold music), Voice (makes the music dip while it plays), Hold music (used by hold zones; dips under voices).

What can trigger a cue?

  • At time — fires once when the timeline reaches a time.
  • Block state — fires when a block changes to a chosen state (e.g. the train leaves the launch).
  • Track trigger — fires when the train passes a track trigger you placed in NL2 (name it audio_trigger_name).
  • On audio restore — fires when the player restores audio in the station (see Stall fade-out).
Block state cues and trainsA block-state cue only fires for your train (the Train index you set). Another train setting off the same block won't trigger it.

8 · Clip & sound-trigger properties

Clip properties

Select a clip on the timeline to edit it in the right-hand Properties panel.

SettingWhat it does
FilenameThe audio file this clip uses. Rename it here to point the clip at a different file (the name must match a file in your audio folder).
AudioRead-only: the file's duration and whether it's Mono or Stereo.
End trim / lengthThe clip's played length (HH:MM:SS:FF). Type an exact length, or drag the clip's right edge. Reset restores the full source length.
TrackWhich track the clip sits on. Organisation only — it doesn't change the in-game mix.
Start timeThe clip's exact start on the timeline (HH:MM:SS:FF).
Start in regionThe clip's offset from the start of the cue that contains it. A read-only helper — it shows "not inside any region" when the clip isn't inside a cue.
VolumeThe clip's gain, 0–100%.
Volume automationOpen the curve editor to shape the clip's volume over time. This is an authoring aid — the game plays the clip's overall Volume as a constant gain.
Play modeOnce plays the clip a single time; Loop repeats the clip while its cue plays.
ColorThe clip's colour on the timeline. Cosmetic only.

Sound-trigger properties

A sound trigger is a single sound fired the moment its track trigger is hit — no time window, unlike a cue. Add one with the + in the Sound Triggers panel (right side, under Properties), then select it to edit:

SettingWhat it does
Name (trigger)The track trigger that fires this sound. In NL2, name the marker audio_trigger_<name> — or exactly the name you type here.
Audio filenameThe file to play.
Play modeOnce for a one-shot (e.g. a scream); Loop to repeat while active.
VolumeThe sound's gain, 0–100%.
Sound trigger vs cueA cue is window-based and plays the clips inside its time range. A sound trigger ignores time entirely — it fires one file directly on the trigger. Use sound triggers for one-shots (screams, horns); use cues for anything timed or looping.

9 · Common tasks

Add audio / create clips

Drag .wav/.ogg files onto the timeline, or use More → Add audio. Clips land where you drop them.

Trim a clip

Drag the clip's right edge to make it shorter, or type an exact length in the clip's End trim / length field (format HH:MM:SS:FF). Click Reset to restore the full length.

Create a cue

Cues menu → +. In Properties, give it a name, choose Triggered by, and pick a Cue type. A Clip Cue plays every clip whose start falls inside the cue's Start–End window — clips at the same position layer together, later ones play in sequence.

Play the audio for every train (independent)

By default a timeline targets one train. To run it for every train independently — each train playing its own copy of the cues as it passes — open Settings → Project and tick All trains (each independent). The Train index box is then greyed out. One script handles the whole coaster, so you don't need separate scripts per train.

Station loops that stop on launch

  • One or more Loop-mode cues triggered by a track trigger you place in NL2 (e.g. audio_trigger_station) — these are your station loops.
  • A Stop Cue with the same trigger as the launch, and a Fade time (e.g. 3 s).

Hold music (music that plays while the train is stopped)

Make a Clip Cue triggered by a Block state and tick Hold music zone. Put the hold music as clips inside that cue's window. When the train stops there for the set time, the ride music fades out and the hold music fades in; when the train leaves, it fades back out.

  • Stopped for — how long the train must be stopped before hold music starts.
  • Hold fade-in / fade-out — the fade times.
  • Ride music fade-out — how fast the ride music fades away.
  • Restore ride music on departure — tick to bring the ride music back when the train leaves.
  • Set the cue's Play mode to Loop for continuous hold music, or Once for a single announcement.
Hear it firstThere's no in-editor preview for hold music, but you can press Export region audio to render the cue's clips to a WAV and listen to it outside the game.

Volume automation (fade a clip in or out)

Select a clip → Volume automation → Edit curve. Click to add points, drag to move, double-click to delete. This shapes the clip's volume over time (an editing aid; the game plays the clip's overall Volume).

Ducking (music dips under a voice)

Set a voice cue's Role to Voice. While it plays, all Music cues dip by the amount set in Settings → Ducking, then recover.

Stall fade-out (auto fade-out when a train gets stuck)

What it's for. While a train is moving, the looping music (station loops, ride music set to Loop) keeps playing until something stops it. If the train stalls — a valley, a mid-course brake, the lift — nothing stops those loops, so they play on and on. Stall fade-out watches for that and fades the audio out, so a stuck train goes quiet. (One-shot sounds end by themselves; it's the loops that would otherwise never stop.)

In one sentence: after a chosen point on the ride, if the train sits still for a set number of seconds, the audio fades out so a stalled train goes quiet instead of looping forever — and it can stay silent until the player restores it at the station.

Setting it up (the essentials):

  1. Tick Fade out when stalled to turn the feature on.
  2. Choose the Arm block and Armed state — the point on the ride where watching begins. "Armed" simply means "start watching from here". A good choice is the block just after the launch, so the station (where the train normally waits) isn't counted as a stall.
  3. Set Stalled for — how long the train must be stopped before the fade starts (e.g. 15 s). Brief stops won't trigger it.
  4. Set Fade-out time — how slowly the music fades away (e.g. 3.5 s).

What happens when it triggers. Once armed, the train is watched. If it stays basically stopped for Stalled for seconds, everything currently playing fades out over Fade-out time — not just loops; one-shot sounds fade out too. Then, depending on Stay muted until restored:

  • Ticked (mute lock): the ride goes completely silent. To bring the music back, the train must be stopped in the Restore block (usually the station) and the player presses the Action key near the rear of the train — like an operator resetting the audio.
  • Unticked: the loops just stop; the next station trigger restarts the music on its own.

The remaining settings (all optional):

SettingWhat it does
Arm block / Armed stateWhere watching starts (see step 2 above).
Stalled forSeconds stopped before the fade begins (see step 3 above).
Fade-out timeHow long the fade takes (see step 4 above).
Stay muted until restoredOn = silent until the player restores it; off = the loops just stop and restart at the next station.
Restore block / Restore stateWhere the Action key restores audio (usually the station / the "in station" state).
Restore cueA cue started automatically when audio is restored (optional).
Restore soundA short sound played on restore, e.g. a beep (optional).
End-watch triggerA track trigger that stops watching for the rest of the ride (optional).
Disable-fade triggerA track trigger that turns the fade off for the current ride (optional).
Quickest setupTick Fade out when stalled; set Arm block/Armed state to the block just after your launch; set Stalled for ≈15 s and Fade-out time ≈3.5 s. For the "press Action to restore" behaviour, also set Restore block to the station and Restore state to the "in station" state.

3D audio distance

Settings → 3D audio sets how sound falls off with distance: reference distance and rolloff (defaults 3 and 5).

Save and reopen your work

More → Save Project saves everything and the audio into one .nl2audio file — a self-contained project. More → Open Project reopens it, no relinking needed.

Import a .bin (layout only)

More → Import .bin loads a timeline you exported earlier. It restores the layout — clips, cues, triggers and settings — but not the audio, so the clips come back blank. Point the editor at your sounds with More → Locate audio folder and the waveforms return. To reopen a full project with its audio, use Open Project instead.

10 · Export & deploy to NoLimits 2

Easiest way — one buttonMore menu → Export Audio Package. It bundles everything the game needs — the game file, the script, the resource list and all your audio — into a single .zip. Unzip it into your park's RideSync folder and you're ready.

Then, in NL2, attach the script and place your triggers (steps below).

Exporting the files individually (optional)

You can also export each file on its own — handy when you only changed one thing and want to replace just that file:

Export buttonFile producedWhat it's for
Export game file (.bin)timeline.binThe data the game reads (changes whenever you edit the timeline).
Generate resource listTimelineAudio.nl2scriptLists your audio so NL2 loads it (changes when you add or remove audio).
Generate game scriptTimelineAudio.nlvmThe script that plays the audio.

Where the files go

YourPark/
└── RideSync/
    ├── TimelineAudio.nlvm
    ├── TimelineAudio.nl2script
    ├── config/
    │   └── timeline.bin
    └── your audio files (.wav / .ogg)
  1. Unzip the package (or copy the three files plus your audio) into that folder.
  2. In NL2, attach the script to the coaster using the .nl2script (not the .nlvm).
  3. Place the track triggers your cues expect, named audio_trigger_name (e.g. audio_trigger_station). The app also matches a trigger with the exact name you typed.
  4. Make sure your block names and states match what you set in the cues.
  5. Run the ride and listen.
Keep them in syncWhenever you change the timeline, re-export and replace the file(s) in the park — easiest with Export Audio Package. Mixing an old script with a new file is the most common cause of “nothing plays”.

12 · Troubleshooting

ProblemThings to check
No audio in-game at all Did you re-export the .bin and regenerate the .nlvm and replace both? Is the script attached via the .nl2script? Are the audio files in the same folder as the script?
One sound never plays Check its cue's Triggered by: is the track trigger named audio_trigger_name (or the exact name you typed) and actually placed? Is the block/state reached? For block cues, is the coaster in the matching scripted/normal mode?
Loops don't stop Add a Stop Cue with the same trigger as the event that should stop them, and give it a Fade time. Fade time 0 means an instant stop.
Export says audio files are missing The audio is in memory but the folder moved. Use Locate audio folder to re-point at your sounds, then export again. Files you can hear on the timeline will be packed.
Clips are blank / waveforms missing after opening Opening a saved project or importing a .bin restores the layout but not the audio — the app doesn't know where the sounds live. More menu → Locate audio folder, pick the folder with your sounds; the waveforms appear on the clips.
Hold music keeps looping / never stops Set the hold cue's Play mode to Once for a single announcement. Make sure the train actually leaves the zone to trigger the fade-out.
Audio doesn't fade when the train stalls Check Settings → Stall fade-out is enabled; that the Arm block/Armed state is actually reached by your train; and that the coaster is in the matching scripted/normal mode. To restore, the train must be stopped in the Restore block and the player presses Action.
Music doesn't dip under voices Give the voice cue Role → Voice and the music cues Role → Music; adjust the dip in Settings → Ducking.
“Timeline won't load” in NL2 Version mismatch: re-export the .bin and regenerate the .nlvm together — always keep them a matching pair.
App won't update / a build fails Close the running app before rebuilding — Windows can't overwrite a running program.
Reading the game consoleNL2 prints helpful lines from the script, such as Cue 'Loop1' fired by trigger. or skipped: … (different train). If a sound doesn't fire, the console usually tells you why.

14 · FAQ

Do tracks or groups change the in-game mix?
No. Tracks and groups are only for organising your work in the editor.

How many cues can I have?
Plenty for a normal ride, but keep an eye on the counts: the game engine supports up to 200 clips, 50 cues and 50 sound triggers. The editor warns you if you go over, and any items beyond a limit are dropped from the exported file — they simply won't play.

Will my project open on another PC?
Yes — Save Project packs the audio into the .nl2audio file, so it's self-contained.

How do I update the ride after editing?
Re-export the .bin and regenerate the .nlvm, then replace both in the park folder.

15 · About the author

RideSync is made by Pieter H., an independent audio engineer working live concerts and studios in France.

I've been rebuilding Rock'n'Roller Coaster in NoLimits 2 since the day the sim came out back in 2014 — starting from scratch, no plans, no blueprints, just photos found online. The community kept coming through with real schematics and actual ride audio from Disney, which was incredible and also meant rebuilding everything from the ground up on a better basis more than once. The ride may not be the prettiest, but its complexity — different audio per train, the rock 'n' roll atmosphere, the lighting — is exactly what sparked something in me and bridged my audio-engineering passion with roller coasters for good.

The reason RideSync exists is a bit different. Rock'n'Roller Coaster's onboard system is genuinely complex, and the old way of working meant bouncing between tools: open the DAW, export, script it, test in-game, then go back to the DAW to adjust — over and over. I wanted that loop to be straightforward, so I built the tool I wished I had. It also means NL2's built-in audio no longer needs coding skill — RideSync doesn't, and reproducing what it does in a few clicks would take you days by hand.

RideSync is built for NoLimits 2, and it will be released publicly — an .exe with no installation required. It'll keep growing from there, with new features and tweaks based on what the community asks for.
