RideSync — Reference Manual

The technical companion to the User Guide: how the editor, the binary format, the engine script and the game fit together. For step-by-step usage, see the User Guide.

1 · Overview

NoLimits 2 can't play audio timed to a moving train on its own. RideSync provides it:

  • A desktop editor (Electron app): tracks, clips, cues, sound triggers and settings.
  • A binary format (timeline.bin, version 7) that stores the whole timeline.
  • An engine script (TimelineAudio.nlvm) that reads config/timeline.bin at runtime and plays the right sounds per train.

The editor authors; the .bin is the single source of truth the game reads; the engine script is a player that only changes when the format changes.

3 · Core concepts

Clip — a piece of audio on a track. Clips are editor-only: they are the material a Clip Cue plays; the engine never starts a clip on its own.

Track — a lane. Tracks, colours, mute, solo and groups are editor-only organisation; the game mixes each cue independently.

Cue — the real playback unit. One cue = one sound event, launched by one trigger. Stored as a CUE record.

  • Clip Cue — plays every clip whose start lies in the cue's [Start, End) window, at their offsets. Clips at the same offset layer; later ones play in sequence. Loop cues wrap and replay; once cues play through and stop. (Sentinel filename __CLIPS__.)
  • Stop Cue — empty filename. On launch, fades out and stops looping cues.
  • File Cue — plays one audio file.

Sound Trigger — a named one-shot sound fired directly by a track trigger. Stored as a TRIGGER record.

Role — per cue: Normal (0), Music (1, ducked by voices and faded by hold music), Voice (2, ducks music for its length), Hold music (3, ducks under voice but is not faded by the ride-music fade).

Hold music zone — a block-triggered Clip Cue whose clips are played as hold music when the train is stopped in that block.

Stall fade-out — an automatic fade of looping audio when the train stalls, optionally with a mute lock until the player restores audio.

Ducking — a separate multiplier applied on top of each cue's fade gain, so a voice never cancels an in-progress fade.

Follow actions — a per-cue list of cues started when this one finishes (once mode; never on manual stop/fade).

Loop crossfade — a per-cue seconds value (v7). A looping cue with a crossfade > 0 blends two handles across the loop seam.

4 · What plays in-game

The rule: cues play, clips do not. A layout like "idle music at 0–20 s, launch at 20 s, station at the end" is authored as clips, then wired to cues the engine launches. If a sound does not fire in-game, check the cue's trigger, not the clip.

5 · Launch triggers

Triggered byBin codeFires when
At time0The timeline clock reaches the cue start (once per crossing)
Block state1A block's state changes to the target — only when this train is on that block's section
Track trigger2The matching audio_trigger_<name> trigger is passed (the engine also matches a trigger with the exact name)
On audio restore3The stall fade-out system restores audio (Action key)

Block matching has two modes (Settings → Blocks → Scripted coaster): scripted (custom states from Block.getState()) or normal-mode states plus Section conditions (works without a scripted coaster).

6 · Behaviors

Ducking

When a Voice cue starts, Music and Hold music cues duck to depth over attack, then recover over release — a separate multiplier on top of each cue's fade.

Hold music

A block-triggered Clip Cue with Hold music zone ticked. On the block transition it arms (does not play). When the train is stopped for Stopped for seconds, the ride music (Music) fades out over Ride music fade-out while the cue's clips fade in over Hold fade-in. On departure the hold music fades out over Hold fade-out; if Restore ride music on departure is ticked, the ride music fades back in. Once-mode completion suppresses follow actions.

Stall fade-out

Armed by a block state; if the train stays below ~0.1 m/s for Stalled for seconds, looping audio fades over Fade-out time. With Stay muted until restored, audio stays muted until the player presses Action in the restore block (optionally playing a restore sound). A disable-fade trigger suppresses the fade for the current ride.

7 · File formats

7.1 timeline.bin (version 7)

Little-endian. Full layout in Appendix A. A 4-byte magic header is followed by the version byte, counts, duration, audio-dir and reserved fields, then clips, cues, sound triggers and the stall-fade-out footer. Version history: v4 adds the audio-dir string; v5 the hold-music timing tail; v6 follow actions + restore cue; v7 the loop crossfade. The engine and the bin must stay in sync — re-export the bin and regenerate the .nlvm together.

7.2 .nl2audio (project)

A zip with project.json plus an audio/ folder holding every referenced file ("collect all and save"). Stores duration, fps, tracks, groups, clips, cues, sound triggers, stall-fade-out settings and per-clip volume automation.

7.3 TimelineAudio.nl2script

Declares each .wav/.pkf as a <resource> so the engine can load sounds by runtime filename. Regenerate it whenever you add audio.

7.4 .nlvm

The compiled engine script (filename must equal the class name). Generated from the editor's built-in engine template, with the class name and the compile-time train index substituted from your settings.

9 · Volume automation

A clip can carry a gain curve (points of {t, gain}, gain 0–200% relative to the clip start). Open it from the clip inspector (Volume automation → Edit curve); click to add, drag to move, double-click to delete. The curve is drawn on the clip and drives the preview gain. It is an authoring aid — the game plays the clip's constant Volume.

10 · The engine

TimelineAudio reads config/timeline.bin in onInit() (parse, load audio, register triggers/blocks, set up stall fade-out), then drives everything from onNextFrame(): advance the timeline clock, fire time/block/trigger/restore cues, run Clip Cue playback, ducking, hold music, and the stall fade-out state machine, and handle loop crossfades.

Sounds load through the NL2 resource system; identical filenames are shared. Console lines (e.g. Cue 'Loop1' fired by trigger.) are the primary debugging tool.

11 · Workflows

Hand-built timeline

  1. New; set length and train index.
  2. Add audio (clips).
  3. Add cues, choose the trigger and role.
  4. Add a Stop Cue to fade loops on launch.
  5. Configure stall fade-out, ducking, 3D audio.
  6. Export the .bin, generate the resource list and script, deploy.

12 · Deployment

YourPark/
└── RideSync/
    ├── TimelineAudio.nlvm
    ├── TimelineAudio.nl2script
    ├── config/
    │   └── timeline.bin
    └── your audio files (.wav / .pkf / .ogg)
  • The .nlvm filename must equal the class name.
  • Attach the script to the coaster via the .nl2script.
  • Every referenced audio file must be declared as a resource in the .nl2script.
  • Re-export the bin and regenerate the .nlvm together after changes.

Export Audio Package bundles the bin, the script files and all audio into one zip.

13 · Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause
Nothing playsBin and .nlvm out of sync; script not attached via .nl2script; audio not in the folder.
One sound never firesWrong trigger name / block / state; trigger not placed; block cue fired by a different train.
Loops don't stopNo Stop Cue, or fade time 0 (instant) vs expected fade.
Hold music won't stopTrain hasn't left the zone; or once/loop play mode not as intended.
Export reports missing audioFolder moved — use Locate audio folder; anything audible on the timeline is packed.
Blank clips after importImporting a .bin restores layout only — relink the audio folder.
Timeline won't load in-gameVersion mismatch — re-export bin + regenerate .nlvm together.

Appendix A · timeline.bin byte reference

Little-endian, version 7.

Header (24 bytes)

OffsetSizeField
04Magic header
41Version (7)
51Track count
62Clip count (u16)
82Cue count (u16)
102Sound-trigger count (u16)
122Footer length (u16)
144Duration (f32)
182Audio-dir length (u16) — v4+
204Reserved

Base header is 18 bytes; for v4+ the clip section begins at 24 + audioDirLen.

Clip record

SizeField
1Track index (u8)
4Start time (f32)
4Duration (f32) — v3+; 120 default for older
1Play mode (0 once, 1 loop)
4Volume (f32)
1+nFilename (len + UTF-8)

Cue record

SizeField
1+nName (len + UTF-8)
1Trigger (0 time, 1 block, 2 track trigger, 3 restore)
1+nBlock name (len + UTF-8)
2State value (u16)
1+nTrack trigger (len + UTF-8)
1+nFilename (len + UTF-8) — empty = Stop Cue; __CLIPS__ = Clip Cue

Footer (length at header offset 12)

Enabled, arm block, arm state, stalled time, fade time, mute lock, restore block, restore state, restore trigger, duck depth/attack/release, confirm sound, disable-fade trigger, audio ref distance/rolloff (v5+), train index, script name, restore cue (v6+), scripted-blocks flag.

Appendix B · Limits & defaults

Editor

  • Timeline length: 10–3600 s. Frame rates: 24/25/30/48/60 (default 30).

Engine caps

  • Clips ≤ 200, cues ≤ 50, sound triggers ≤ 50. The editor warns on export if exceeded.

Defaults

  • Stall fade-out: off; arm block state, stalled 15 s, fade 3.5 s, mute lock on.
  • Ducking: depth 0.75, attack 0.35 s, release 0.8 s.
  • Hold music: stopped 10 s, fade-in 2.5 s, fade-out 10 s, ride fade-out 2 s, state 8.
  • Cue: volume 1.0, fade 0.5 s, once. Clip: volume 1.0, once.
